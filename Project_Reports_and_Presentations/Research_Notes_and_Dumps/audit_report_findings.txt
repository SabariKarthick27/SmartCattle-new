5. CHAPTER 5 / RESULTS AND DISCUSSION AUDIT

P[400]: In the present chapter, architecture, mathematical formulation and implementation of the QR-Based Cattle Management System were presented. It's built with Next.js, FastAPI, PostgreSQL and QR passports that can be accessed via a smartphone to handle cattle information and run four physiological parameters through the CHDI engine. Explainable health monitoring and a 7-day data-sufficiency gate support along with personalized 14-day baselines and mahalanobis distance and LOCO attribution to prevent false alarms. Role-based dashboards for farmers, veterinarians and administrators allow for cattle management, health monitoring and automated clinical triage. The active-learning pipeline is supported by validated veterinary diagnosis and treatment success, which boosts the performance of the Random Forest classifier.5. CHAPTER 3 – RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
P[401]: 5.1 Introduction
P[402]: The chapter provides the evaluation findings of the proposed livestock management system which includes animal identification based on QR, health scoring of animals based on CHDI, and alert generation to veterinarians.
P[403]: 5.2 Experimental Setup
P[404]: The platform was tested on a benchmark cow population of 16 Holstein-Friesian cows during a monitoring period of 90 days with 674 multi-signal observations that were synchronized (Zenodo multimodal benchmark / published reference distributions). The database contained 42 clinical disease events (acute mastitis, metabolic ketosis) for anomaly detection performance assessment.
P[405]: 5.3 Evaluation Metrics
P[406]: System evaluation follows standard statistical metrics:
• Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN)
   Measures the proportion of true clinical disease anomalies successfully detected by the system.
•  Specificity = TN / (TN + FP)
   Measures the system's resistance against false alarms during normal healthy baseline days.
• Precision = TP / (TP + FP)
   Measures the proportion of generated alerts that correspond to genuine clinical distress.
•  F1-Score = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall)
   The harmonic mean balancing precision and sensitivity.
•  Lead Time = 2.4 Days Advance Warning
   Lead time between initial sub-clinical CHDI deviation and observable clinical symptoms.
P[407]: 5.4 Experimental Results
P[408]: The engine for anomaly detection was tested on 674 synchronized observation records for an anomaly window of 90 days from 16 Holstein-Friesian cows using the Four-Signal Composite Health Deviation Index (CHDI). Body Weight, Feed Intake, Core Body Temperature and Daily Milk Yield were assessed as the four core parameters monitored in this study using the animal's personal 14-day rolling historical baseline as the reference.
P[409]: In the 674 benchmark observation records, the proposed system was trained to classify the cattle health in four severity bands as shown in Figure 5.1.
